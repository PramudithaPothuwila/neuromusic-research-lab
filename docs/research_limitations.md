# Research limitations

1. **Recording independence.** 56 of 87 files are copies (some relabelled as different
   participants or songs) or synthetic. Only 30 recordings from 22 participants are treated as
   independent. Until the data owner explains how files SRC036–SRC074 and SYNTH* were produced,
   participant counts above 22 must not be claimed.
2. **Design.** Most participants heard one song, so song and participant effects are largely
   confounded. Within-participant tests are not possible on the current data; the
   between-recording fallback has low power (≈ 2–5 participants per song, 9 testable songs).
3. **Many classes, few samples.** 16 songs with 1–5 recordings each makes subject-independent
   song classification near-impossible; expect chance-level results. Songs with a single
   recording can never be predicted correctly under LOSO.
4. **Units and reference unknown.** All amplitudes are arbitrary units; asymmetry and regional
   measures depend on the unknown reference.
5. **Device and montage unverified.** No topographic maps are produced.
6. **Timing and coverage.** Recordings start at song onset (owner statement), but onset
   precision is undocumented and recordings cover only the first 21–151 s of 157–440 s songs.
   Inter-subject correlation is limited to the shortest recording per song and to songs with
   ≥ 2 independent participants (9 songs; 2–5 participants each).
7. **Confounds not recorded.** Presentation order, fatigue, familiarity, volume, baseline state,
   movement, electrode contact over time.
8. **Song attributes** are song-level aggregates of unknown provenance, not participants' own
   ratings; associations have n = number of songs.
9. **Stimulus identity.** Confirmed by owner statement only (2026-10-29); no acquisition log.
10. **Interpretation.** Spectral differences are not measures of emotion; classification of the
    studied songs does not imply generalisation to new songs.
